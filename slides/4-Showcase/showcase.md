### Relevant Examples
- **Awaze**: Automated "tidying" of property images and masking PII.
- **Carnival Cruises**: Using NotebookLM to synthesize strategy and reorganize spa menus.
- **Condé Nast (Vogue)**: Classification AI to label thousands of fashion attributes (e.g., "Is this a bouffant dress?").

Note: 
- Awaze: Replaced manual PII masking (car plates) and clutter removal with an AI pipeline, forecasting 50% cost savings.
- Carnival: Compressed days of document synthesis into hours using RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) tools like NotebookLM.
- Condé Nast: Used 'Classification'—one of the five core AI question shapes—to sort massive datasets with binary precision.

---
### Lessons Learned
- Impact over Complexity
- The 3-week Proof-of-Concept
- Signal vs. Noise (Bronze to Gold)

Note: The biggest lesson across all these is that the most complex AI isn't always the most valuable. The "Quick Wins" often come from simply taming the administrative 'toil'. Success depends on moving data from "Bronze" (noise) to "Gold" (signal) before you start the PoC.
